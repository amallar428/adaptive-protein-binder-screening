The approach

Modern structure-prediction models can provide useful information about candidate binders, but repeated evaluations across large design libraries are computationally expensive. We are studying when additional predictions are worth their cost.

01 / EVALUATE

Measure prediction value

Study how structure-prediction models and repeated runs contribute distinct information about binder candidates.

02 / ALLOCATE

Use compute selectively

Investigate decision policies that reserve additional evaluations for candidates where they may change screening decisions.

03 / VALIDATE

Benchmark rigorously

Compare computational selection quality and prediction costs against simple fixed-budget baselines, with explicit limits on what the available data can establish.

Research & infrastructure

The project is in a feasibility and prototyping phase. Its focus is reproducible evaluation—not claims of experimentally validated binders or a deployed drug-discovery product.

Current focus

Adaptive structure-prediction evaluation

We are assessing the data and benchmarking requirements for computational binder-screening workflows, including the value of complementary predictors and repeated inference.

  • Candidate-level prediction and ranking benchmarks
  • Comparisons against fixed prediction schedules
  • Explicit accounting of GPU inference cost
Planned compute

GPU-accelerated inference

GPU resources would support benchmarking open research models for biomolecular structure prediction, potentially including Boltz and related predictors, subject to feasibility, access, and model terms.

Results will be interpreted within the limits of the evaluated datasets and models.

About the project

Adaptive Protein Binder Screening is an early-stage, independently presented research and prototyping initiative led by an MIT undergraduate working at the intersection of computer science and molecular biology. It is not represented as an official MIT program or an operational biotechnology service.
